 PART A - WHERE THE SKY FITS                                         12 marks
=============================================================================

 A1. The sky is drawn by one call in one function. Name the function it is
     called from, and state its position relative to everything else drawn in
     that function. Quote the surrounding two or three lines. (3)

 A2. The skybox uses its own shader program, not the main one. Explain where
     that program is created and why it needs to be separate. Your answer
     must name at least two things the main shader does that the sky shader
     has no use for. (4)

 A3. skybox.vert computes a value it passes to the fragment shader. Say what
     that value is and what makes it unusual compared with every other vertex
     shader you have seen in this course. (3)

 A4. skybox.frag has no lighting, no normal and no shadow lookup. Explain why
     none of them would make sense for this object. (2)

=============================================================================
 PART B - THE CUBE MAP, AGAIN                                        16 marks
=============================================================================

 B1. The Skybox constructor builds a cube map. Compare it, call by call, with
     OmniShadowMap::Init from Activity I. Produce a two-column table of the
     differences. There are at least four. (6)

 B2. One of those differences is the filtering mode. Explain why each project
     chooses the mode it does. Your answer must say what is being stored in
     each case. (4)

 B3. The upload loop asks the image loader for a number of channels, and the
     upload itself names a pixel format. Find both. State whether they agree
     for the files that ship with this project, and explain what would happen
     if someone replaced one face with an image that had an alpha channel.
     You do not have to try it. (4)

 B4. If a face file is missing, the constructor prints a message and returns
     early. Describe the state the object is left in and what the program
     does afterwards. Say whether this is handled well. (2)

=============================================================================
 PART C - THE THREE MECHANISMS                                       24 marks
=============================================================================

 Skybox::DrawSkybox is short. Three things in it, plus one thing about where
 it is called from, are what make a small cube look like a distant sky.

 C1. Identify all three, and for each one write a single sentence saying what
     it does. (6)

 C2. One of them converts a matrix to a smaller matrix and immediately back
     again, which looks like it achieves nothing. Write out the structure of
     a 4x4 view matrix, say which part of it holds the rotation and which
     part holds the translation, and from that explain exactly what survives
     the round trip and what is destroyed. (6)

 C3. PREDICT, in writing, what you would see if the line from C2 were removed
     and you then walked forward continuously. Be specific. Then explain why
     TURNING the camera would be unaffected by the same removal. (5)

 C4. Test C3. Report whether you were right and correct yourself if not. (3)

 C5. The depth mask is switched off before the draw and on again after.
     Explain what would happen to the floor and the pyramids if both lines
     were removed. Your answer must involve the SIZE of the sky cube. (4)

=============================================================================
 PART D - THE CUBE ITSELF                                            16 marks
=============================================================================

 D1. The cube is eight corners written out as literal numbers. Each line
     holds more numbers than a 3D position needs. Say how many there are per
     corner, what each group is for, and why the extra ones are all zero in
     this particular object. (4)

 D2. The mesh is created with two integer arguments after the two arrays.
     Work out what each number counts and show the arithmetic that produces
     it from the data above. (4)

 D3. The index list draws twelve triangles. Pick any one face of the cube,
     identify the two triangles that cover it, and state which way they are
     wound when seen from INSIDE the cube. (4)

 D4. Face culling is never enabled anywhere in this project. Say what that
     means for your answer to D3, and say what would have to be true about
     the winding if culling were switched on. (4)

=============================================================================
 PART E - HOW SMALL, HOW BIG                                         20 marks
=============================================================================

 Arithmetic and reasoning. The camera sits at the exact centre of the sky
 cube - establish that from your Part C answer before you go on.

 E1. Find the camera's projection and state its field of view, near plane and
     far plane. Note that the bulbs have a near plane too, and it is a
     different number - say which one is which and why they are unrelated. (4)

 E2. For a cube of half-size s centred on the eye, write down two distances:
     from the eye to the centre of a face, and from the eye to a corner.
     Derive the second one. (4)

 E3. Using E1 and E2, derive the SMALLEST half-size at which the sky is still
     visible. State which part of the cube crosses which plane first, and
     give the number. (4)

 E4. Derive the LARGEST half-size at which the WHOLE sky is still visible.
     State which part fails first and give the number. (4)

 E5. The cube ships at half-size 1. Explain why any value between your two
     limits produces an identical picture. Your answer must explain why no
     visual cue can reveal the difference, and should mention both the matrix
     from Part C and the depth mask. (4)

=============================================================================
 PART F - TEXTURE UNIT ZERO, REVISITED                               12 marks
=============================================================================

 In Activity I you found a texture unit that was deliberately left empty. In
 this project the sky is bound to that same unit and the sampler that reads
 it is never assigned a unit at all.

 F1. Find the line that binds the sky, and quote it. (2)

 F2. Find where, or confirm that nowhere, the skybox shader's sampler is
     given a unit number. State what unit it therefore uses. (3)

 F3. Explain why relying on that default is safe here when the same default
     was a fault in Activity I. Your answer must say what else is bound to
     that unit in each of the two cases. (5)

 F4. Quote the line in main.cpp that keeps the unit free for the main shader.
     (2)

=============================================================================
 PART G - ONE JUDGEMENT                                              10 marks
=============================================================================

 G1. The Skybox class allocates two objects on the heap. Find them. Then look
     at the destructor. State what happens to those allocations when a Skybox
     is destroyed. (4)

 G2. A Skybox is default-constructed as a global and later assigned from a
     temporary. Trace what happens to the two pointers during that
     assignment. Say whether the program works, and whether it is correct.
     Those are two different questions. (6)

=============================================================================
 WHAT GOOD WORK LOOKS LIKE
=============================================================================
 The marks are for showing that you found something in the code and
 understood why it is written that way.

 A strong answer:
   - cites file and function every time
   - quotes only the line that matters
   - explains a mechanism, not a label
   - reports a wrong prediction honestly and says what was misunderstood

 A weak answer:
   - renames the code in prose ("glDepthMask turns off the depth mask")
   - cites nothing
   - answers Part E by measuring instead of deriving

 Parts C3 and E are where you commit to an answer before checking it. Those
 are marked on the reasoning. A wrong prediction, honestly corrected, is
 worth more than a right one with no working.
